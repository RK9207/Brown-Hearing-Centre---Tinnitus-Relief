### B. Problems, Conditions & Symptoms

**Sounds listed by the site** (S3): ringing, buzzing, whooshing, hissing, clicking.

**Other symptom details stated** (S3):
- Sounds are heard only by the person experiencing them, though sometimes detectable by a hearing professional.
- Sounds may be constant or intermittent.
- Some people have trouble sleeping because they feel their brain never "shuts off."

**Causes listed by the site** (S3): exposure to loud noise, medication, hearing loss, ear infection, trauma to the ear, earwax buildup, Meniere's disease, vascular disorders. The site notes tinnitus can result from one or more of these, and some causes (e.g., infection, earwax buildup) can be resolved easily if temporary.

**Emotional impact mentioned** (S3): the site references emotions ranging from anxiety to anger in connection with behavioral therapy.

**Related hearing-loss signs** (S11 — useful because hearing loss is a listed tinnitus cause): hearing mumbling, asking people to repeat, turning the TV/radio up louder than others, missing doorbells/phones, avoiding noisy restaurants, missing environmental sounds. S11 also notes it takes people an average of seven years to seek treatment for hearing loss (unsourced on page).

### D. Treatment / Service Process

**Tinnitus-specific steps documented on the site** (S3): (1) provider assesses severity, symptoms, daily-life impact, and possible causes; (2) provider recommends one of: addressing the underlying problem, hearing aids with masking features, white noise machines, or TRT.

**General hearing-care process documented** (S9 — "Finding the Perfect Match"):
1. Come in for a hearing test; professional determines if hearing aids are the best solution.
2. Get matched with a device suited to hearing loss, lifestyle, and budget.
3. Customize and optimize — device is tested and adjusted for the right fit.
4. Regular check-ins for cleanings, maintenance, and follow-up care.

**Hearing evaluation details** (S10):
- Performed by a hearing instrument specialist in their office.
- Consists of a variety of tests determining aspects of hearing loss and the level at which speech can be detected and understood.
- Typically lasts **up to one hour**, plus time to review results and ask questions.
- Bringing a family member is recommended.
- If hearing aids are recommended, the specialist discusses styles (in-the-canal, in-the-ear, behind-the-ear) and features (S10 lists Bluetooth, directional microphones, and masking features for noisy environments — *note: this "masking" refers to noisy environments, not tinnitus*).
- Results are plotted on an audiogram (S11).

**Online pre-screening** (S9): free, 5-minute online hearing screening at https://brownhearingcenters.com/survey/hearing-survey/.

**Follow-up** (S7, S9): ongoing follow-ups, maintenance, cleanings.

**Preparation / recovery:** **[MISSING]** — not documented for tinnitus.

### H. Limitations & Disclaimers

- **Scope of practice:** Brown's providers are Hearing Instrument Specialists. The site's own FAQ describes the HIS role as basic hearing testing for hearing aid candidacy, fitting, and counseling (S11). Several treatment options on the tinnitus page (medication changes, antibiotics, behavioral therapy, earwax removal, possibly TRT) are typically delivered by physicians, audiologists, or mental-health professionals. The page does not say Brown provides them. **Do not imply medical diagnosis or medical treatment of tinnitus.** [INFERRED risk — client confirmation required]
- **No cure claim on the site.** The site speaks of "relief," "masking," and "easing symptoms," not cures. (S3)
- The tinnitus page is educational and generic (2022). It does not describe a Victoria-specific service.
- Remote testing accuracy may vary depending on the patient's system (S17).
- The hearing evaluation's masking reference is for noisy environments, not tinnitus (S10).
