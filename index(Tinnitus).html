TINNITUS & HEARING CARE

How hearing care may help with tinnitus

Tinnitus is ringing, buzzing or other sounds you hear without an outside source. Different causes call for different approaches.

Understand your hearing

A Hearing Instrument Specialist checks which sounds you hear and how well you understand speech. Your results are shown on a chart called an audiogram and explained to you.

Hearing loss can be associated with tinnitus. Understanding your hearing can help guide the next conversation.

Where hearing aids may fit

Some hearing aids have tinnitus-masking features that may make unwanted sounds less noticeable, including for some people without hearing loss. Results vary, and suitability needs individual assessment.

Ask the Victoria office which options are available.

Some causes of tinnitus need medical care.

A hearing evaluation assesses your hearing; it isn’t a medical diagnosis. Speak with your physician about medical concerns.

What to expect when you visit

From your first call to discussing your results, here’s how to get started.

  1. Contact the Victoria office

    Call or request an appointment online. Tell the team about the ringing, buzzing or other sounds you’re hearing.

  2. Have your hearing checked

    Your Hearing Instrument Specialist checks which sounds you hear and how well you understand speech, then explains your results.

  3. Understand your options

    Discuss whether hearing aids may help and which options fit your needs and budget. Ask about tinnitus-masking features available at Victoria.

  4. Fitting and follow-up

    If you choose hearing aids, they’re fitted and adjusted for you, with follow-up for cleaning, maintenance and ongoing support.

Plan for up to an hour for the evaluation, plus time to discuss results. Bringing a family member is recommended. Recommendations depend on your hearing results and individual needs.
