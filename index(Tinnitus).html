How hearing care may help with tinnitus

Tinnitus is sound you hear when nothing around you is making it. People describe it in different ways:

  • Ringing
  • Buzzing
  • Whooshing
  • Hissing
  • Clicking

It can be constant or come and go. Some people find it hard to sleep because it feels like their mind never switches off. Tinnitus has several possible causes, so a useful first step is understanding what’s happening with your hearing.

A hearing evaluation

What it is

A Hearing Instrument Specialist runs a series of hearing tests to see which sounds you hear clearly and how well you understand speech. Your results are plotted on a chart called an audiogram and explained to you.

Why it matters

Hearing loss is one of the possible causes of tinnitus. Knowing whether it’s involved helps you make an informed decision about what to try next, instead of guessing.

Hearing aids with tinnitus-masking features

What it is

Some hearing aids can be equipped with features designed to help cover the ringing or buzzing. These features may help even if you don’t have hearing loss.

Why it matters

Making the sound less noticeable may bring relief during your everyday routine. Results vary from person to person, and whether a device suits you depends on your evaluation.

Fitting, fine-tuning and follow-up

What it is

If hearing aids are recommended and you choose them, they’re tested and adjusted for your fit. Regular check-ins cover cleaning, maintenance and follow-up care.

Why it matters

Your devices can be adjusted over time, so you have support after the first appointment rather than being left to figure things out alone.

Some causes of tinnitus need medical care

Tinnitus can be linked to loud noise, certain medications, hearing loss, ear infections, earwax buildup, injury to the ear, Meniere’s disease or vascular disorders. Some of these, such as an infection, call for medical treatment. A hearing evaluation focuses on your hearing and isn’t a medical diagnosis. If your tinnitus may have a medical cause, your physician is the right person to evaluate it.

What happens when you contact us

Here’s how a first visit usually unfolds, so you know what to expect before you call.

  1. Call or request an appointment

    Call 409-231-6980 or send an appointment request online. Let the team know you’re hearing ringing or other sounds in your ears.

  2. Have your hearing evaluated

    Your Hearing Instrument Specialist runs a series of hearing tests. A standard hearing evaluation typically takes up to an hour, plus time to review results and answer your questions. Bringing a family member is recommended.

  3. Talk through your results and options

    You’ll see your results on an audiogram and discuss what they mean. If hearing aids may help, including models with tinnitus-masking features, you’ll talk through styles and features suited to your hearing, lifestyle and budget.

  4. Fitting and ongoing care

    If you choose hearing aids, they’re tested and adjusted for your fit. Regular check-ins follow for cleaning, maintenance and follow-up care.

Every recommendation depends on your evaluation results and your individual needs.
