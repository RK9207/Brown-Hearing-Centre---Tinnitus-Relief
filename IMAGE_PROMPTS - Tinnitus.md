## Project Visual Strategy Summary

**Clinic:** Brown Hearing Centers, Victoria office (5606 N Navarro St #200). The company has been family owned since 1962, when its first office opened in Victoria. The provider is Nick Yendrey, M.B.A., Hearing Instrument Specialist (HIS). He is not an audiologist or a physician.

**Topic:** Tinnitus relief. The page positions the Victoria office as a clear local starting point: a hearing evaluation, then hearing-care options that *may* help, such as hearing aids with tinnitus-masking features. Local availability of those features is still unconfirmed.

**Target audience:** Adults in Victoria and the surrounding area who notice ringing, buzzing or hissing in their ears. They are realistically skewed toward people in their 50s to 80s. Captured Google reviewers describe first-time hearing-aid users, spouses attending together, and an adult child bringing an elderly parent. The audience should reflect Victoria's mixed Anglo and Hispanic community naturally, without stereotypes such as cowboy-hat or ranch clichés.

**Primary visitor intent (inferred):** Local, action-oriented. "Is there someone near me who can help with the ringing in my ears, and how do I start?" The secondary intent is symptom recognition ("what is this noise?").

**Approved visual personality:** Professional, warm, familiar, local and deliberate (Design System §1). Avoid three things:
* Luxury exclusivity
* Futuristic product marketing
* Anything that implies hospital or physician credentials

**Visual storytelling arc (Page Strategy §10):** warm human opening → quiet service explanation → accountable local identity → structured next steps → visible proof → calm answers → practical contact.

**Primary role of imagery:** Imagery plays two roles on this page:
1. **Human recognition in the hero.** The visitor should see themselves in it.
2. **Authentic proof of local identity in the provider and location sections.** These show a real person, a real history and a real door to walk through.

Everything else is carried by typography, structure and the verified reviews. FAQs, process steps and the reviews section deliberately have no imagery.

**Real vs AI balance:** The page is real-first. Three of the four placeholders exist to prove authenticity: the provider portrait, the archival heritage photo and the Victoria office entrance. For those, AI imagery is a weak fallback at best, and sometimes counterproductive. Only the hero can reasonably use AI-generated editorial photography. Design System §11 states that *no AI imagery is required*.

**Major authenticity and accuracy rules applied throughout:**
* No AI-generated person may appear in a slot labelled with Nick's name or presented as Victoria staff.
* No AI image may imitate an archival photograph or depict the Victoria building.
* No hearing aid shown may suggest a specific brand, a tinnitus feature or a guaranteed outcome.
* No AI image may depict audiometric equipment or a test procedure, since the Victoria setup is undocumented.
* No AI image may contain signage, logos or visible text.

**House photographic look:** This applies to every AI option, so the set feels like one shoot.
* Documentary-editorial realism
* Soft natural window light or late-afternoon South Texas light
* Warm cream and honey-wood highlights, muted denim and steel blues, deep navy shadows
* Low-to-moderate saturation and natural skin texture
* 35–85mm lens feel and subtle fine grain
* No HDR, no glossy stock-photo polish

This look sits comfortably beside the brand palette (#10639d blue, #051c3b navy, #fdf4ed cream) without forcing brand colors into clothing.

## IMG-HERO-01 — Section 1: Service and Location Hero

### 1. Placeholder purpose

**Section:** Hero, right column on desktop (beside the H1 "Ringing in your ears? Find tinnitus help in Victoria, Texas.", the supporting copy, the Call and Request buttons, the Google badge at 4.9 from 52 reviews, and the trust line). On mobile the image sits **after** all text, CTAs and trust signals.

**Conversion objective:** Confirm relevance within seconds and make the visitor comfortable enough to call.

**What the image must communicate:** "This page is about someone like me, and about something I'm noticing." Design System §14 describes the hero's role as *"Human opening"*, with the narrative transition *"Recognition prompts explanation."* The image should create quiet recognition. It should not sell a product or promise a result.

**Why imagery is valuable here:** Text alone carries the topic, location and action. The image adds human warmth and emotional permission ("this is common, and it's okay to ask about it"). Text does that less efficiently.

**Relationship to the copy:** The H1 asks a symptom question. The lead invites the visitor to "Talk with our Victoria team." The image should sit between those two ideas: a person living an ordinary life who is noticing something.

### 2. Recommended source type

**Either real or AI image could work.**

The ideal is an authentic environmental photograph taken at the Victoria office, such as Nick in conversation with a consenting patient or welcoming someone at the front desk. That would combine human warmth with local proof. No such image exists yet (RESEARCH §9, items 17 and 18).

Because the hero's job is recognition rather than documentation, a well-art-directed AI editorial image is legitimately suitable, provided it shows the *visitor's* world and not Brown's premises or staff.

### 3. Recommended composition

**Orientation / Aspect Ratio:** Generate at **4:5 vertical**. The desktop container is 4:5. The mobile container is **4:3 landscape**, cropped from the same file with `object-fit: cover`, which removes about 40% of the height.

**Primary Subject:** One adult aged 55–70, in or near an everyday home setting.

**Subject Placement:** Centered to slightly right. Keep the face in the upper-middle of the frame.

**Negative Space:** No text overlays this image, so it doesn't need text-safe space. Leave calm space above and left of the head so the image doesn't feel crowded against the headline column.

**Camera / Perspective:** Eye level, roughly 50mm feel, shallow depth of field, mid-distance (waist-up or seated three-quarter).

**Lighting:** Soft, warm window light or late-afternoon light from camera left, which faces toward the headline column on desktop.

**Color Direction:** Warm cream, honey wood, muted blue. These echo the cream hero background (#fdf4ed), so the image blends into the section rather than reading as a pasted-in photo.

**Desktop Crop:** Full 4:5. The subject's face and hands should sit in the central 60% of the frame's height.

**Mobile Crop:** In the 4:3 crop, the face and the main gesture must survive inside the middle horizontal band. Keep nothing important in the top or bottom 20%.

**Visual Priority:** The face and expression, then the sense of an ordinary, comfortable place.

## Overall Image Strategy Review

### A. Visual cohesion

All AI options share one photographic look: warm natural light, cream/honey/denim-blue/navy palette, low saturation, fine grain, documentary-editorial realism. That keeps them consistent with each other and with the cream and navy page.

The one deliberate exception is IMG-PROVIDER-01 Option 3, an illustration. That's a reason it is not recommended.

### B. Storytelling progression

The intended sequence works:
1. **Hero:** human recognition (the visitor's world).
2. **Provider:** the real accountable person.
3. **Heritage:** real history.
4. **Location:** the real door.

This moves from "someone like me" to "someone real, here, for a long time" to "I know where to go," which mirrors Page Strategy §10. The middle sections stay image-free by design, giving the visual relief Design System §14 calls for.

### C. Image variety

The recommended set is varied: one lifestyle portrait, one headshot, one archival photo, one exterior. The risk is in the fallbacks. If real assets don't arrive, three of four slots fall back to still lifes or atmosphere, and IMG-PROVIDER-01 and IMG-HERITAGE-01 would put two desk still lifes in one section. **Rule: use at most one AI fallback in Section 3.**

### D. Authenticity

This is the defining issue for this page. Three of four slots (provider, heritage, clinic) are **real-image-dependent**. Their AI fallbacks were written to avoid misrepresentation, but each is a weaker substitute.

For IMG-HERITAGE-01 and IMG-CLINIC-01, the honest recommendation is **remove the slot rather than fill it with AI**. For IMG-PROVIDER-01, use the existing real headshot, which is the single most important image action.

### E. Content alignment

The images support the approved messages without overreaching:
* No image implies tinnitus relief, a cure or a test result.
* Hearing aids appear only in generic, unbranded form in provider fallbacks, never as a tinnitus solution.
* No audiometric equipment is shown, because the Victoria setup is undocumented.

### F. Search-intent alignment

The hero recommendation (Option 1) matches the inferred problem-aware, local intent: symptom recognition paired with a local next step. The local-intent visuals are exactly the real-image slots, which is why sourcing them matters.

### G. Conversion relevance

Each slot has a genuine job:
* Hero: recognition.
* Provider: accountability.
* Heritage: longevity proof.
* Clinic: wayfinding.

None is decorative when filled with real imagery. Heritage and clinic become largely decorative when filled with AI, which is the basis for the removal recommendation.

### H. Responsive practicality

* **Main risk:** IMG-HERO-01's 4:5 to 4:3 mobile crop. All three hero prompts keep faces in the central band, and a dedicated mobile crop via `<picture>` is recommended.
* **Smaller risk:** IMG-CLINIC-01 renders about 197px tall on mobile, so signage must be large in frame.
* **Section 3 on mobile:** stacking the provider portrait (4:5), bio, HIS box, buttons and heritage image (1:1) makes it long. Codex may consider hiding the heritage image under 600px in Step 5.

### Concerns and redundancies for Step 5

1. Retrieve and confirm usage rights for Nick's headshot. Request a higher resolution than 600×800.
2. Retrieve and inspect PICTURE-1, and confirm the caption with the client.
3. Ask the client for a daylight photo of the Victoria entrance, or pull owner-uploaded GBP photos.
4. Decide whether to remove IMG-HERITAGE-01 and/or IMG-CLINIC-01 if real assets aren't available.
5. If a real Victoria environmental photo of Nick becomes available, it may be a better hero than AI Option 1. Avoid using Nick's headshot in both the hero and Section 3.
